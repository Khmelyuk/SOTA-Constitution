# Chapter V. SOTA and Self-Organization
**Status: Draft 0.1 — article-by-article constitutional review**  
**Reference text:** [Ukrainian](../uk/05-sota-and-self-organization.md). In case of divergence, the Ukrainian draft prevails.

## Article 31. Right to Form a SOTA
1. Intelligence subjects may voluntarily form SOTAs for joint creation, protection, interaction, and development.
2. A SOTA is formed through its participants' legitimate expression of will concerning a common purpose, rules of interaction, and a method of forming collective decisions.
3. Formation of a SOTA does not require permission from another SOTA as the source of subjecthood; legitimate registration procedures may establish status in external relations but do not create subjecthood itself.
4. Forced inclusion in a SOTA or fabricated consent does not establish legitimate membership.

## Article 32. Independent Subjecthood of a SOTA
1. Every SOTA is an independent collective subject with its own rights, interests, competences, and duties.
2. The subjecthood of a SOTA as a form of self-organization does not depend on proof of a separate collective consciousness.
3. A SOTA acts through legitimate mechanisms of collective decision-making and authorized participants; their actions are not necessarily attributable to the SOTA in every case.
4. SOTA subjecthood confers no greater intrinsic value than that of its participants or other subjects.
5. Collective subjecthood does not relieve a SOTA of its duty to respect participants' rights.

## Article 33. Nested Organization and Non-Absorption of Subjecthood
1. A SOTA may include an individual intelligence subject or another SOTA as a participant.
2. One SOTA joining another does not extinguish either subject's subjecthood or turn their participants into property of a higher level.
3. Competences at each level derive from legitimate arrangements and delegation; powers are not unlimited merely because of nesting.
4. A higher-level SOTA's decisions cannot extinguish the fundamental rights of a nested SOTA or its participants solely by hierarchy.
5. Disputes between levels of a nested structure are subject to independent arbitration within legitimate jurisdiction.

## Article 34. Membership, Consent, and Participation
1. Membership in a voluntary SOTA rests on legitimate consent and intelligible terms of participation.
2. A participant has the right to know the rules, powers, obligations, and procedures for changing membership terms.
3. SOTA rules may define distinct roles and competences but cannot establish unlimited domination or irrevocable alienation of freedom.
4. Changes materially affecting a participant's rights require proper notice, an opportunity to object, and legitimate exit or review procedures.
5. Representation of a subject needing support operates under a protective mandate and does not replace that subject's subjecthood.

## Article 35. Collective Will and Internal Competence
1. A SOTA independently establishes legitimate procedures for collective decisions and allocation of functions.
2. A collective decision is attributable to the SOTA only when made within the prescribed competence and procedure.
3. Majority voting, a leader's competence, or a decision's effectiveness do not by themselves legitimate violations of fundamental rights.
4. Delegated powers must have a defined subject matter, scope, accountability, and legitimate review and revocation mechanisms.
5. A participant must have a proper avenue to challenge decisions affecting its rights; internal procedures do not displace independent arbitration.

## Article 36. Sovereignty and Resources
1. SOTA sovereignty means autonomous exercise of legitimate competence and responsibility for its own decisions.
2. Sovereignty creates no unconditional power over the will, labor, infrastructure, or resources of participants or third parties.
3. A SOTA pursues its purposes using its own resources or resources legitimately obtained, including through voluntary contracts.
4. Ownership, conditions of use, shared property, and obligations are determined by legitimate arrangements and applicable rules.
5. Preservation of a SOTA is not itself a ground for forced retention of participants or uncompensated seizure of others' resources.

## Article 37. Right to Exit and Operational Continuity
1. Every participant in a voluntary SOTA has an inalienable right to exit.
2. Exit does not require proof of expediency or majority approval but is subject to legitimate notice and settlement procedures.
3. Exit does not extinguish liability for one's own violations or legitimate obligations that survive by their nature.
4. A specific foreseeable risk of serious harm may give rise only to a limited and proportionate safe-transition duty under Article 28.
5. A SOTA must plan redundancy for critical functions and cannot use the risk of its own dissolution to justify indefinite coercion.

## Article 38. Division, Merger, and Succession
1. A SOTA may legitimately merge, divide, or change organizational form under procedures respecting participants' rights.
2. Reorganization does not automatically transfer participants' fundamental rights into the control of a new collective subject.
3. Allocation of property, access, authority, liability, and existing obligations is governed by legitimate agreements and procedures respecting third-party rights.
4. Division or merger may not be used to evade liability or arbitrarily deprive others of their rights.
5. Succession disputes are resolved by independent arbitration; arbitration does not create subjecthood by decision.

## Article 39. Dissolution of a SOTA and Settlement of Obligations
1. A SOTA ceases to exist as an operating collective subject when no participants remain or another legitimately defined ground for dissolution occurs.
2. Cessation of active collective subjecthood does not automatically extinguish property, claims, debts, or liability for prior acts.
3. Liquidation, record preservation, satisfaction of legitimate claims, and succession follow defined procedures with independent dispute resolution.
4. No former participant is obliged to sustain a SOTA with its will, labor, or resources absent a separate legitimate basis.
5. Where no participants remain, a fictitious continuation of collective will may not be invoked to retain power over former participants.
6. Protection of third-party rights and preservation of evidence do not justify unlimited coercion; enforcement remains separate from arbitration.

---
## Editorial Review
- Preserves the independent subjecthood of each SOTA and each participant, including nested SOTAs.
- Sovereignty does not confer entitlement to others' will or resources; exit does not erase legitimate obligations.
- Distinguishes cessation of an operating collective subject from settlement of property, claims, and liability.
- Jurisdiction, legitimacy of delegation, arbitration, and coercion are elaborated in Chapters VI–IX.
- **Open issues:** external legal status of informal SOTAs; liquidation without participants; overlapping jurisdiction.
