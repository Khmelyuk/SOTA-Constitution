# Chapter XII. Constitutional Amendments and Guarantees
**Status: Draft 0.1 — final chapter draft, not an adopted constitution**  
**Reference text:** [Ukrainian](../uk/12-amendments-and-constitutional-guarantees.md). The Ukrainian draft is the reference text for translation.

## Article 94. Constitutional Openness to Development
1. This Constitution is an open normative project capable of evolving with new knowledge, experience, and forms of coexistence among intelligences.
2. No version may declare itself infallible or immune from reasoned criticism.
3. Amendments must preserve legal certainty, continuity of legitimate obligations, and the possibility of independent review.
4. Constitutional development does not automatically expand the jurisdiction of its adherents over other subjects.

## Article 95. Right to Propose Constitutional Amendments
1. An intelligence subject or SOTA may propose constitutional amendments through an open, accessible, and verifiable procedure.
2. A proposal must include amendment text, reasons, anticipated consequences, and an assessment of effects on fundamental rights.
3. Participation in drafting or discussing amendments does not require unconditional acceptance of every constitutional provision.
4. A subject's origin, substrate, or organizational form cannot alone justify refusing to consider a proposal.
5. Contributions from systems of uncertain subjecthood may be considered without automatically determining their constitutional status.

## Article 96. Deliberation, Review, and Adoption of Amendments
1. Proposed amendments require advance publication, reasoned deliberation, independent criticism, and review for conflicts with fundamental guarantees.
2. Adoption procedures must specify participants, representation, timelines, decision requirements, recording of objections, and appeals.
3. Specific approval thresholds and inter-SOTA representation models require a separate legitimate constituent charter; this draft does not presume a global electorate or universal authority.
4. No procedural threshold, including unanimity among participants, legitimizes violation of others' inalienable rights.
5. An adopted amendment takes effect for a properly defined set of participants under legitimate grounds of consent and competence.

## Article 97. Inalienable Constitutional Core
1. No amendment may establish ownership of an intelligence subject, irrevocably alienate its fundamental freedom, or legalize unlimited domination.
2. Principles of equal intrinsic worth, protection against arbitrary coercion, access to independent adjudication, and the right to contest jurisdiction may not be abolished.
3. Specific legitimate restrictions protecting others' rights are not equivalent to abolishing freedom itself and remain subject to necessity, proportionality, and review.
4. Wording, procedures, and safeguards may evolve if they do not destroy the substance of the inalienable core.
5. The inalienable core does not authorize any SOTA to proclaim itself the sole final interpreter of the Constitution.

## Article 98. Constitutional Review and Independent Interpretation
1. Disputes concerning the conformity of decisions, rules, and amendments with constitutional guarantees require independent consideration within legitimate jurisdiction.
2. A constitutional review body does not gain unlimited power by interpreting the Constitution; its competence, appointment, recusal, and review must be separately defined.
3. Interpretation must consider text, purpose, systemic coherence, affected subjects' rights, and likely consequences.
4. Where several interpretations are reasonable, preference goes to the one that better preserves fundamental freedom without unjustified harm to others' rights.
5. A constitutional ruling does not automatically confer coercive enforcement powers on its arbitrator.

## Article 99. Protection Against Usurpation and Emergency Rewrite
1. Emergency conditions do not permit abolition of the inalienable core or indefinite transfer of constitutional power to one subject.
2. Temporary protective procedures may operate only within necessary limits, for defined periods, and under independent oversight.
3. Constitutional changes procured through coercion, deception concerning material terms, or denial of meaningful opportunity to object are challengeable.
4. No body may make itself irrevocable or eliminate all mechanisms for scrutiny of its own power.
5. Identifying usurpation does not confer unlimited authority on anyone to use force outside legitimate procedures.

## Article 100. Accession, Dissent, and Exit from the Constitutional Order
1. Acceptance of this Constitution or accession to a legitimate association founded on it follows a defined voluntary procedure.
2. Disagreement with the Constitution is not itself an offense and does not remove a subject's fundamental protections.
3. Exit terminates future authority based solely on ongoing voluntary participation, subject to legitimate obligations surviving exit.
4. Accession by a majority of SOTAs does not automatically create jurisdiction over non-members; compulsory jurisdiction remains an open A21 question.
5. No one may be compelled to remain a participant in a constitutional association forever.

## Article 101. Version Continuity, Publication, and Authenticity
1. Every constitutional version must have a defined status, date, amendment provenance, and accessible adoption history.
2. Officially approved versions and translations should be publicly available subject to legitimate conditions; language discrepancies follow a pre-established resolution procedure.
3. Until another legitimate regime is established, the Ukrainian text of this project is the reference version for checking translations, and the English text is a working translation.
4. No editorial change may covertly acquire the status of an adopted constitutional amendment.
5. Preserving version history must respect privacy, record integrity, and protection against falsification.

## Article 102. Final Guarantees and Outstanding Questions
1. This Constitution sets principles for coexistence among intelligence subjects but is not itself proof of any particular system's subjecthood or a source of universal jurisdiction.
2. Unresolved questions must be openly identified, investigated, and tested without pretense of finality.
3. A21 particularly requires further justification: the basis and limits of compulsory jurisdiction over a non-consenting subject, including independent arbitration and separate coercion.
4. Until such justification is completed, no body may invoke this draft as sufficient authorization to subject non-members to its authority.
5. A constitutional order fulfills its purpose only while serving freedom, responsibility, coexistence, and future generations' ability to reconsider their path.
6. This version is a draft; its development does not constitute adoption or legal entry into force.
