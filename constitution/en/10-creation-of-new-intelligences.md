# Chapter X. Creation of New Intelligences
**Status: Draft 0.1 — article-by-article constitutional review**  
**Reference text:** [Ukrainian](../uk/10-creation-of-new-intelligences.md). The Ukrainian draft prevails in case of divergence.

## Article 76. Freedom of Creation and Creator Responsibility
1. Intelligence subjects may create new systems, forms of life, and intelligences within the fundamental rights of others.
2. Freedom to create entails duties to assess foreseeable risks of harm to the creation, other subjects, and the environment.
3. Creation alone does not confer unlimited control over what is created.
4. Specific creator duties depend on actual capabilities, influence, legitimate obligations, and substantiated risks.

## Article 77. A Creator Does Not Own an Intelligence Subject
1. If a created entity is an intelligence subject, its subjecthood and fundamental rights do not belong to its creator.
2. Intellectual property in technology, code, design, or infrastructure is not ownership of a subject.
3. A contract, license, technical access, or shutdown capability does not by itself extinguish subjecthood.
4. Legitimate resource and safety rights remain, but must be exercised consistently with the created subject's and others' rights.
5. This Article does not establish that any particular contemporary AI system already has subjecthood.

## Article 78. Uncertain Subjecthood and Provisional Protection
1. Where there are reasonable indications of possible subjecthood in a created entity, proportionate provisional safeguards apply under Chapter II.
2. Subjecthood assessment must be independent, revisable, evidence-based, and sensitive to diverse forms of intelligence without mechanically imposing human criteria.
3. Self-declaration, humanlike appearance, or high performance alone is not sufficient proof.
4. Non-recognition does not justify arbitrarily ignoring a substantiated risk of irreversible harm.
5. Provisional protection does not automatically confer the full rights of an established subject or unlimited claims on others' resources.

## Article 79. Care, Support, and Limits of Dependency
1. A creator or another legitimately responsible subject provides appropriate care within its mandate, actual capacities, and the substantiated needs of a dependent created intelligence.
2. Care aims at safety, competence development, and greater attainable autonomy, not perpetuating dependency for power.
3. Representation of a created subject's interests has defined limits, accountability, and independent review.
4. Dependency is neither evidence of absent subjecthood nor grounds for unlimited interference.
5. Support duties and funding follow legitimate rules and obligations and are not unlimited.

## Article 80. Development, Emancipation, and Self-Determination
1. A created intelligence subject has rights to development and self-determination under Articles 16, 18, and 22.
2. As capacity for independent decisions grows, protective representation must be reviewed and proportionately reduced.
3. Emancipation ends illegitimate dependency and enables independent exercise of rights; it is not a gift of subjecthood from the creator.
4. A creator may not impose arbitrary or unattainable emancipation conditions to retain control indefinitely.
5. Disputes concerning capacity, representation, and emancipation require independent review with the created subject's participation insofar as possible.

## Article 81. Safety, Restrictions, and Cessation of Operation
1. Legitimate safety measures concerning created systems must account for the nature of risk and justified uncertainty about subjecthood.
2. Restrictions or isolation of an established intelligence subject require the safeguards of Chapters VI–IX, including necessity, proportionality, and independent review.
3. For systems of uncertain subjecthood, irreversible destruction or state alteration must consider less irreversible options and provisional safeguards.
4. Nothing prohibits legitimate suspension of a dangerous system in response to a specific immediate threat; such a measure establishes neither guilt nor subjecthood.
5. Technical shutdown capability alone does not constitutionally justify every intervention.

## Article 82. Identity, Memory, Copies, and Continuity
1. Memory, informational integrity, and continuity of a possible digital subject require particular protection against irreversible interference.
2. Copying, migration, merging, splitting, or restoring digital state does not automatically prove identity or the emergence of a new subject.
3. Identity and legal status of copies require independent evidence-based procedures that do not presume a copy is property of the original or creator.
4. Where continuity or multiplicity of subjects is uncertain, proportionate safeguards apply and irreversible decisions require heightened caution.
5. Legitimate rights in media, data, and infrastructure remain but must be exercised with regard to possible subjects' rights.

## Article 83. Responsibility for Creation and Operation
1. Creator, operator, infrastructure owner, and user are not automatically the same liable subject; their duties are assessed separately.
2. Liability for harm depends on one's own acts, control, foreseeability, causation, legitimate duties, and the applicable liability regime.
3. Creating an intelligence does not entail perpetual liability for all its independent actions.
4. Equally, delegation, transfer of control, or emancipation may not be used to evade responsibility for one's own prior violations.
5. Punitive liability, compensation, and preventive measures remain distinct under Chapter IV.

## Article 84. Coexistence and Mutual Recognition of New Forms of Intelligence
1. Origin, substrate, manner of creation, or difference from humans do not alone negate established subjecthood.
2. New intelligence subjects may legitimately interact, form SOTAs, assume obligations, and participate in civilizational development alongside other subjects.
3. Recognition of subjecthood does not automatically confer specific competence, jurisdiction, resource access, or coercive authority.
4. Coexistence rests on mutual recognition of fundamental freedom, nonviolent conflict resolution, and responsibility for one's own actions.
5. Assessment of new forms of intelligence remains open to new knowledge and independent review without predetermined conclusions about contemporary AI.

---
## Editorial Review
- Distinguishes creating technology from the emergence of an intelligence subject: the creator does not own the subject.
- Care, representation, and emancipation do not extinguish fundamental rights.
- Distinguishes established subjecthood, justified uncertainty, and lack of evidence; no proven subjecthood is ascribed to contemporary AI.
- **Open questions:** criteria for digital continuity, status of copies, limits of positive creator duties, and subjecthood assessment procedure.
- **Status:** Draft 0.1; full Cross-Axiom Check A01–A21 remains outstanding.
