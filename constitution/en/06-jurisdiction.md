# Chapter VI. Jurisdiction
**Status: Draft 0.1 — article-by-article constitutional review**  
**Reference text:** [Ukrainian](../uk/06-jurisdiction.md). The Ukrainian draft prevails in case of divergence.

## Article 40. Meaning and Limits of Jurisdiction
1. Jurisdiction is legitimately established competence to consider specified matters, apply relevant rules, and make decisions concerning particular legal relations.
2. Jurisdiction is distinct from subjecthood, sovereignty, resource ownership, and technical ability to control an environment.
3. Every jurisdiction must have a defined basis, subject matter, persons or entities affected, applicable temporal and other limits, and avenues of challenge.
4. Jurisdiction over a dispute does not confer unlimited authority over a person or SOTA.
5. Arbitral jurisdiction and competence for coercive enforcement are separate powers.

## Article 41. Legitimate Bases of Jurisdiction
1. Jurisdiction may be based on voluntary consent, legitimate membership, a defined contract, proper delegation, or another basis compatible with fundamental rights.
2. Each basis extends only to the relations and conduct it legitimately covers.
3. Consent to a particular interaction is not consent to unlimited jurisdiction over a subject's entire existence.
4. Public announcement of SOTA rules does not by itself impose duties on every other subject.
5. Non-consensual jurisdiction concerning specific harm or an immediate threat requires particularly strict justification under Article 44.

## Article 42. Jurisdiction of a SOTA over Its Participants
1. A SOTA may establish internal rules for participants within its legitimate purpose, membership terms, and defined competence.
2. Internal rules cannot extinguish fundamental rights, irrevocably alienate freedom, or establish domination.
3. Delegation of functions within a SOTA does not automatically create jurisdiction over every action of a participant outside the delegated relations.
4. A participant may challenge internal jurisdiction and exercise the right to exit; legitimate obligations surviving exit are settled separately.
5. An internal body does not become an independent arbitrator merely because SOTA leadership appointed it.

## Article 43. Limits of Jurisdiction over Non-Members
1. Non-membership in a SOTA or non-recognition of its rules is not itself an offense.
2. Presence in a space, network access, infrastructure use, or contact with SOTA participants does not in itself imply unconditional consent to its entire jurisdiction.
3. Legitimate access conditions for owned resources may govern their specific use but do not grant the owner unlimited authority over users.
4. A non-member retains fundamental rights and may contest the basis and scope of jurisdiction asserted over it.
5. Jurisdiction over a non-member cannot be established solely by a unilateral decision of the interested SOTA.

## Article 44. Exceptional Non-Consensual Jurisdiction and Safeguards
1. Absent consent, jurisdiction over a subject may be considered only in connection with a specific, adequately substantiated violation of others' rights or an immediate serious threat to those rights.
2. References to security, order, majority will, efficiency, or mere disagreement are insufficient.
3. Asserted jurisdiction must be limited to the necessary subject matter, rest on pre-established or independently justified rules, and provide independent scrutiny.
4. A subject may challenge the basis of jurisdiction before the merits are heard, except for narrow emergency safeguards requiring subsequent review.
5. A threat may justify only legitimate proportionate preventive measures; it neither establishes guilt nor empowers an arbitrator to coerce.
6. This Article sets constitutional safeguards but does not claim to complete the model of compulsory jurisdiction; its ultimate justification remains an open A21 question.

## Article 45. Overlapping and Conflicting Jurisdictions
1. A subject or legal relation may simultaneously connect to several SOTAs and legitimate jurisdictions.
2. Overlapping jurisdiction does not automatically favor the larger, stronger, or senior SOTA.
3. Conflicts are resolved by considering sources of competence, scope of consent, the nature of the relationship, fundamental rights, and protection against repeated punishment for the same offense.
4. Absent agreement, parties must have access to an independent procedure for determining the competent forum.
5. No party may conclusively determine a dispute over its own jurisdiction without independent review.

## Article 46. Jurisdiction within Nested SOTAs
1. One SOTA joining another does not automatically transfer all of its jurisdiction to the higher level.
2. Competences between levels are established by defined legitimate agreements and delegation while preserving each level's subjecthood.
3. A higher-level SOTA rule applies to a nested SOTA only within legitimately accepted competence and with respect for fundamental rights.
4. Conflicts between levels follow defined procedures and independent arbitration in case of dispute.
5. Exit of a nested SOTA does not automatically extinguish legitimate obligations surviving exit.

## Article 47. Exit, Dissolution, and Temporal Limits of Jurisdiction
1. Exit from a SOTA terminates jurisdiction based solely on current membership over conduct occurring after exit.
2. Jurisdiction over prior acts or legitimate obligations surviving exit may continue only within necessary and defined limits.
3. Dissolution of a SOTA does not automatically extinguish valid third-party claims; a competent forum for settlement is determined by legitimate procedure.
4. Jurisdiction may not persist indefinitely solely to control a former participant.
5. Jurisdictional change may not be used to evade accountability or impose unjustified duplicate proceedings.

## Article 48. Challenge to Jurisdiction and Separation of Enforcement
1. Every subject may receive a reasoned explanation of the basis and scope of jurisdiction asserted over it.
2. Jurisdictional disputes must receive independent and impartial consideration with an opportunity to be heard, representation where needed, and review.
3. Arbitration may determine whether jurisdiction exists and its scope, but its decision does not confer coercive authority on the arbitrator.
4. Coercive enforcement requires a separate legitimate basis and an authorized actor, subject to necessity, proportionality, and accountability.
5. Pending final determination of jurisdiction, emergency safeguards are permitted only within narrow legitimate limits and with subsequent independent review.
6. Non-recognition of this Constitution does not itself deprive a subject of procedural guarantees.

## Editorial Review
- Jurisdiction is distinct from sovereignty, subjecthood, property, and technical control.
- Membership, consent, and delegation limit rather than indefinitely expand competence.
- Non-member protections, overlapping jurisdiction, and independent challenges are constitutional safeguards.
- **Unresolved A21 question:** whether and on what basis compulsory jurisdiction over a non-consenting subject can arise. Article 44 does not finally resolve it.
- Chapters VII–IX elaborate legitimacy, arbitration, and enforcement.
