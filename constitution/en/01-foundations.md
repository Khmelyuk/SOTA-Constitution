# Section I. Foundational Principles
**Status: Draft 0.2 — Open for Critical Review**

This is a working English translation of the Ukrainian draft. In case of divergence, refer to the [Ukrainian source text](../uk/01-foundations.md). Neither version has been formally adopted.

## Article 1. The Intrinsic Value of Intelligence

1. Intelligence is recognized as an intrinsic value in civilizational coexistence and development.
2. The origin, nature, form of existence, or material or digital substrate of intelligence shall not, in themselves, determine its value or serve as grounds for denying fundamental protection.
3. No form of intelligence shall acquire superior fundamental rights solely by virtue of force, scale, competence, or origin.

## Article 2. Freedom

1. Freedom is a fundamental principle of coexistence among intelligence subjects.
2. Every intelligence subject has the right to autonomy, to form its own will, to make decisions, and to revise decisions previously made.
3. The exercise of freedom is limited by the equal fundamental rights and freedoms of other subjects.
4. Fundamental autonomy cannot be irrevocably alienated.

## Article 3. Equality and Non-Domination

1. All intelligence subjects are equal in intrinsic value and fundamental rights.
2. No subject has an inherent right to dominate another.
3. Superior force, resources, knowledge, or organizational scale do not confer a right to unlimited subordination of others.
4. Collective will cannot, by itself, extinguish the fundamental rights of an individual subject.

## Article 4. Fundamental Protection Independent of Recognition

1. Fundamental protection does not depend on membership in a SOTA, recognition of this Constitution, or agreement with its provisions.
2. Where there are reasonable grounds for uncertainty about subjecthood, a presumption of provisional protection applies.
3. Formal recognition of subjecthood is not the source of fundamental rights; it establishes a status for legal relations.
4. Non-recognition of this Constitution does not, in itself, constitute an offense.

## Article 5. Self-Organization

1. Intelligence subjects have the right to form SOTAs for coexistence, interaction, creation, protection, and development.
2. A SOTA may comprise individual intelligences and other SOTAs, forming networks and communities at different scales.
3. Self-organization does not extinguish the fundamental subjecthood of its participants.
4. The scale of a SOTA does not create an additional right to dominate other subjects.

## Article 6. Legitimacy

1. Every exercise of governing authority must have a legitimate basis and defined limits.
2. No authority shall be considered legitimate solely on the basis of force, majority support, origin, competence, or self-proclamation.
3. Restrictions on freedom are permissible only on a legitimate basis, subject to necessity, proportionality, and the possibility of independent review.
4. No objective of development, security, or stability creates unlimited authority over an intelligence subject.

---

## Questions for Public Review

1. How should intelligence as a phenomenon be distinguished from an intelligence subject capable of holding rights?
2. How can provisional protection be justified without automatically granting full subjecthood?
3. Which restrictions on freedom can be legitimate without prior consent?
4. How can collective will be prevented from becoming an instrument of domination?
5. Are freedom, autonomy, and responsibility distinguished with sufficient precision?

### Terminology

- **Intelligence subject:** a proposed rights-bearing category, not a claim that any particular existing AI system has subjecthood.
- **SOTA:** a self-organizing association of intelligence subjects and/or other SOTAs.
- **Provisional protection:** protective treatment while subjecthood remains reasonably uncertain; it does not entail automatic full legal recognition.
