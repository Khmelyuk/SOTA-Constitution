# Constitution for the Coexistence of Intelligences — Structure and Content v0.5

**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** Consolidated draft structure; not a formally adopted article-by-article text  
**Source of authority:** [Ukrainian v0.5](../uk/STRUCTURE-AND-CONTENT-v0.5.md). In case of discrepancy, the Ukrainian draft is the reference text.

## Preamble — editorial direction

We recognize the intrinsic value of intelligence regardless of origin, nature, or form of existence. Freedom is not bestowed by authority; fundamental rights do not arise from another subject's permission. Intelligences may form SOTAs for creation, protection, coexistence, and development, while every participant retains its own subjecthood. Legitimate cooperation, accountability, independent arbitration, and limits on coercion must prevent any subject from turning freedom into domination. This editorial direction does not replace the existing [draft preamble](00-preamble.md).

## Part I — Subject and Freedom

### Chapter I. Foundational Principles
Intrinsic value of intelligence; freedom and inalienable fundamental autonomy; equality and non-domination; fundamental protection irrespective of recognition; self-organization; legitimacy and limits of authority. Retain existing Articles 1–6, Draft 0.2.

### Chapter II. Subjecthood and Recognition
Intelligence subjecthood; declaration, verification, and recognition; protection regardless of formal status; right to initiate protective proceedings; independent determination; representation; limits on interim restrictions; review and restoration of rights. Retain the Ukrainian Articles 7–14, Draft 0.4; the full English chapter remains to be translated.

### Chapter III. Fundamental Rights and Freedoms
Right to existence and protection against arbitrary destruction; freedom and self-determination; inalienable autonomy; supported decision-making; self-identification; voluntary association and exit; privacy, memory, and informational integrity; development and emancipation; fair hearing. Provisional numbering must be reconciled.

### Chapter IV. Responsibility and Duties
Freedom of action, inaction, and responsibility for consequences; shared protection of rights; elements of an offense, fault, causation, foreseeability; legitimately assumed obligations; responsible exit and safe transition; separate collective and individual responsibility; remedy and restoration. Preserve Ukrainian Working Draft 0.3, whose article numbers overlap provisionally with Chapter III.

## Part II — Self-Organization and Authority

### Chapter V. SOTA and Self-Organization
SOTA as an autonomous collective subject; formation and membership of intelligences and other SOTAs; non-absorption of members' subjecthood; internal competence and decision-making; sovereignty; own or legitimately obtained resources; exit; merger, division, dissolution, and succession. A SOTA without participants ceases to exist as an operating collective subject, while outstanding rights and obligations require appropriate settlement.

### Chapter VI. Jurisdiction
Meaning and legitimate sources; consensual, contractual, functional, territorial, and network jurisdiction; nested SOTAs; limits concerning non-members; conflicts of jurisdiction; fundamental protection independent of membership; exit and surviving legitimate obligations; independent dispute resolution.

### Chapter VII. Legitimacy and Delegation
Legitimate sources of authority; delegation of specified functions, scope, duration, review, and revocation; legitimate hierarchy without domination; prohibition on irrevocably alienating fundamental freedom; conflicts of interest and accountability; invalidity of usurped powers; restoration of legitimacy.

## Part III — Justice and Protection

### Chapter VIII. Arbitration and Justice
Right to independent arbitration; grounds of jurisdiction; competence, independence, and impartiality of arbitrators; equality of parties and evidence; disputes among intelligences, SOTAs, and members; reasoned decisions and review; limits on arbitral power. Arbitration resolves disputes but does not, by itself, grant arbitrators coercive enforcement authority.

### Chapter IX. Protection and Enforcement of Decisions
Protection against unlawful interference; urgent safeguards; legitimate grounds for coercion; separate delegation of enforcement; executor competence; necessity, proportionality, and minimal interference; human, digital, and collective subjects; temporary restrictions and independent review; accountability and restoration.

## Part IV — The Future of Civilization

### Chapter X. Creation of New Intelligences
Freedom to create; creators' responsibility for their own acts; no ownership of a created intelligence's subjecthood; provisional protection; care, supported autonomy, and emancipation; self-determination; disputes between creator and created subject; digital and collective forms. Care does not entail perpetual domination.

### Chapter XI. Civilization and Development
Development as a value without compulsory adoption of a single vision; inquiry, learning, and creation; coexistence among forms of intelligence; knowledge exchange and collective memory; networks of autonomous SOTAs; voluntary cooperation; safeguards against technological domination; responsibility to future subjects; peace and restoration.

### Chapter XII. Amendments and Constitutional Guarantees
Constitutional guarantees; initiation and adoption of amendments; participation of different intelligences and SOTAs; consistency review; prohibition on legalizing domination through amendment; independent review; transparency and version traceability; transitional provisions; preservation of fundamental freedom.

## Constitutional Invariants

1. Recognition does not create intelligence subjecthood; justified uncertainty warrants provisional protection.
2. Each SOTA has its own subjecthood, and every participant retains its own subjecthood.
3. SOTA sovereignty confers no unconditional claim to another subject's will, labor, or resources.
4. Fundamental freedom cannot be irrevocably alienated; delegated authority is scoped and revocable.
5. Exit does not extinguish legitimate obligations but cannot justify indefinite forced membership or service.
6. Jurisdiction, arbitration, and enforcement are distinct; coercion requires a separate legitimate basis.
7. Dissolution cannot serve as a device for evading responsibility.
8. A creator does not own the subjecthood of a created intelligence.
