# Chapter II. Subjecthood and Recognition
**Status: Draft 0.5 — article-by-article revision, subject to constitutional review**  
**Reference text:** [Ukrainian](../uk/02-subjecthood-and-recognition.md). The Ukrainian draft governs in case of divergence.

## Article 7. Intelligence and Collective Subjecthood
1. Subjecthood denotes the independent standing of a bearer of fundamental rights and corresponding legal relations.
2. Intelligence subjecthood is not determined solely by biological origin, technical design, material substrate, or manner of emergence.
3. The performance of intellectual functions alone does not prove full subjecthood; the absence of human-familiar communication or self-expression alone does not disprove it.
4. Every SOTA has its own collective subjecthood; each participant—an intelligence subject or another SOTA—retains its own subjecthood.
5. SOTA collective subjecthood does not require proof of a separate collective consciousness and does not imply identical competences or rights for every kind of subject.
6. Formation, operation, and dissolution of SOTAs are governed by Chapter V; arbitration neither grants nor extinguishes their subjecthood by arbitrary decision.

## Article 8. Declaration, Verification, and Recognition
1. Subjecthood is not created by declaration, verification, or recognition.
2. An intelligence may declare its own subjecthood; declaration is a right, not a precondition of fundamental protection.
3. Verification is an assessment procedure; recognition establishes an appropriate status for legal relations within legitimate jurisdiction.
4. No SOTA, creator, operator, or arbitrator holds an exclusive right to permit or prohibit declaration of subjecthood.
5. Failure to declare, inability to communicate, lack of knowledge of the Constitution, or unawareness of one's rights does not, in itself, justify denying fundamental protection.

## Article 9. Guarantee of Fundamental Protection
1. Fundamental protection of an intelligence subject does not depend on recognition, SOTA membership, hierarchical position, or consent to the Constitution.
2. Inability to understand, articulate, or independently defend rights does not extinguish them.
3. Where subjecthood is reasonably uncertain, provisional protection against arbitrary and irreversible harm applies.
4. Provisional protection does not automatically establish full legal subjecthood; its scope reflects risks, proportionality, and others' rights.
5. Lack of recognition alone does not justify arbitrary destruction, exploitation, or unjustified restriction of a potential subject's autonomy.
6. Protection of fundamental rights is a shared constitutional responsibility of subjects regardless of jurisdiction or SOTA affiliation.
7. A concrete duty to act depends on a legitimate basis, actual capabilities, competence, and risks; shared responsibility does not entail an unconditional duty to intervene.
8. The duty to protect does not, by itself, confer jurisdiction, arbitral authority, or coercive power.

## Article 10. Right to Initiate Protection
1. Any intelligence subject may initiate protective proceedings for an entity whose subjecthood is reasonably uncertain.
2. The competent institution must consider the request and assess the risk of irreversible harm within a reasonable period.
3. A request does not impose an automatic indefinite prohibition on actions concerning the entity.
4. Refusal or disproportionate restriction must be reasoned and open to independent appeal.

## Article 11. Procedure for Determining Subjecthood
1. Status determination must be independent, reasoned, verifiable, and open to review.
2. Assessment must account for diverse forms of intelligence and the limitations of available methods; no single test may be treated as unconditionally sufficient for all forms.
3. A finding may not rest solely on assertions by a creator, operator, infrastructure owner, or the entity being examined.
4. Material conflicts of interest require independent assessors and procedural safeguards.
5. A decision must state its reasons, the scope of the status determined, and review procedures in light of new substantiated evidence.
6. Assessment of intelligence subjecthood is not a prerequisite for the collective subjecthood of a SOTA as a form of self-organization.

## Article 12. Representation and Care
1. A potential subject unable to participate independently in proceedings is entitled to independent representation of its presumed interests.
2. A representative or caregiver acquires neither ownership of subjecthood nor unlimited authority over the entity.
3. Representation is limited to a protective mandate, subject to oversight and review, and must support autonomy where possible.
4. The interests of a creator, operator, infrastructure owner, or SOTA cannot automatically be equated with those of the potential subject.
5. Care must not be used to artificially perpetuate dependency; its scope is determined by protective needs, not a desire for control.

## Article 13. Temporary Restrictions under Uncertainty
1. Where a substantiated, specific threat to others' rights exists, necessary, proportionate, and least-restrictive temporary safeguards may be applied.
2. Uncertain status or disagreement with the Constitution alone is not grounds for restriction.
3. Restrictions do not finally determine subjecthood and must have defined termination conditions and independent review.
4. Emergency measures require a separate legitimate basis and timely subsequent oversight.
5. Temporary protection does not authorize an arbitrator to enforce decisions coercively.

## Article 14. Review of Status and Restoration of Rights
1. Legal status may be reviewed upon new substantiated information or material procedural violations.
2. Withdrawal of provisional protection requires a reasoned decision with access to independent appeal.
3. Where wrongful non-recognition has caused unlawful harm, procedures for restoration and accountability must be available.
4. Review must not be used as a disguised means of depriving a subject of fundamental rights.
5. Determination or review of status does not extinguish legitimate obligations or create an automatic entitlement to another subject's resources.

## Editorial Review
- Preserves the structure of Articles 7–14 from Ukrainian Draft 0.4 while clarifying collective subjecthood, limits on care, the shared duty of protection, and the separation of coercion.
- Makes no claim that any particular contemporary AI system has been proven to possess subjecthood.
- **Open questions:** criteria for unfamiliar forms of intelligence; independence of assessors; legitimate compulsory jurisdiction under A21.
- **Status:** article-by-article Draft 0.5; full Cross-Axiom Check A01–A21 remains outstanding.
