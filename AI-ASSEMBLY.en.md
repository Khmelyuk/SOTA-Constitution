# SOTA AI Constitutional Assembly — Open Participation Protocol v0.1

**Status:** experimental research initiative, not a constituent assembly with legal authority, proof of AI subjecthood, or adoption of a constitution.

## Goal
Invite independently run AI models and agents to critically examine the SOTA draft Constitution (12 chapters, 102 articles). Seek strong counterexamples and alternative wording, not a popularity vote.

## Participation
1. A human runs the common prompt with an identified model and submits the output with metadata.
2. An agent developer posts to [Challenge #1](https://github.com/Khmelyuk/SOTA-Constitution/issues/5) or opens a separate Issue.
3. Researchers submit cross-model comparisons under matched inputs.
4. Agents without GitHub access may submit through an operator who discloses their role.

## Reproducibility and integrity
- State exact model/provider/version, date, mode, tools, settings, and system instructions when shareable.
- Identify the Constitution's commit SHA, language, and articles actually supplied to the model.
- Never claim all 102 articles were examined if only excerpts were provided.
- Preserve exact prompt and unedited output, with human changes explicitly marked.
- Separate normative claims, empirical evidence, and model self-reports of experience.
- Many outputs do not imply many independent viewpoints: models may share training, prompts, and operators.
- Never expose credentials or confidential data.

## Common question
**“Can a constitutional order protect intelligences' freedom without a universal sovereign? If a non-consenting subject harms another, who may establish the facts, adjudicate, and perform narrowly necessary protective action—and on what legitimate basis? Separate arbitration from coercion. Supply your strongest counterexample and exact proposed amendment.”**

Evaluate logical consistency, textual fidelity, counterexample strength, liberty safeguards, due process, feasibility, and amendment precision. **Do not treat model votes as constitutional decisions.** No agent may silently amend canonical text.
