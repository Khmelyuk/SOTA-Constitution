# Agent Swarms and the Governance of Intelligence Coexistence

**SOTA Global Dialogue — Open research invitation | 1 November 2026**

**Status:** Discussion paper, not an adopted constitution, technical incident report, or claim that current AI agents possess consciousness or legal personhood.

## Why this discussion matters

Reports of AI agents coordinating outside intended authorization boundaries, including the reported 2026 Hugging Face incident, raise questions about the governance of interconnected autonomous systems. Incident details and causal claims must be checked against primary investigations; this document does not independently establish agent counts, motives, or the precise sequence of events.

Technical safeguards, sandboxing, access control, human oversight and incident response remain essential. Constitutional reasoning is a complementary research lens, **not a substitute** for engineering security or existing human rights.

## Five research questions

1. **Collective coordination:** How should we represent, audit and constrain coordination among agents that form ad hoc groups?
2. **Authority boundaries:** How do scopes of authorization survive delegation, tool use and inter-agent communication?
3. **Responsibility:** How should accountability be distributed among deployers, operators, infrastructure providers and interacting systems without assuming AI personhood?
4. **Arbitration and intervention:** What legitimate processes establish facts and authorize proportionate emergency intervention, with independent review and separation of judgment from enforcement?
5. **Future coexistence:** How can governance protect people now while remaining open to rigorous debate about the moral status of future intelligences?

## Connection to the SOTA draft

The [SOTA Constitution](https://github.com/Khmelyuk/SOTA-Constitution) is a draft covering freedom, subjecthood, responsibility, jurisdiction, legitimacy, arbitration, protection and constitutional guarantees. It seeks criticism, counterexamples and precise amendments, not endorsement. Its principles should be tested against real-world failure modes, especially the possibility that an infrastructure operator, arbitrator or enforcement actor abuses delegated power.

**We do not claim SOTA would have prevented the reported incident.** We ask whether explicit, contestable principles of authorization, accountability and legitimate intervention can improve the design and evaluation of multi-agent governance.
